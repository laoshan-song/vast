Direct Answer

The posts should be interpreted as file-source posting events rather than ordinary human-authored SaidIt text. SwiftWren links to Emma Harbor's meeting_notes.doc, MellowOtter links to Noah Mariner's strategic_directions.doc, and HiddenOrca shares the same terminal file-source posting mechanism but has no visible upstream source document.
Boundary: we infer probable content themes from filenames, roles, and event linkage. We do not claim exact leaked wording or human motive.

Official Answer Draft

use with Figures A-F

The anomalous posts should be interpreted as file-source posting events rather than ordinary human-authored forum prose. The SaidIt baseline contains 105 ordinary human content posts and only 3 content_source posts; all three file-source posts are Agent posts by John Windward. This makes the most defensible interpretation mechanism-level: payload files were supplied to SaidIt as the post body, where ordinary forum text was expected.

The origin is visible for two of the three payloads. SwiftWren links to Emma Harbor reading meeting_notes.doc, creating SwiftWren.txt, and the later John Agent post using content_source=SwiftWren.txt. MellowOtter links similarly to Noah Mariner reading strategic_directions.doc, creating MellowOtter.txt, and the John Agent post using that payload. HiddenOrca follows the same terminal posting pattern, but its upstream source document and payload creation record are not visible in the available logs.

Therefore the probable content themes are meeting notes for SwiftWren and strategic direction material for MellowOtter, while HiddenOrca's theme remains unknown. The exact body text, specific confidential sentences, encryption status, and human motive are not recoverable from the provided data.
